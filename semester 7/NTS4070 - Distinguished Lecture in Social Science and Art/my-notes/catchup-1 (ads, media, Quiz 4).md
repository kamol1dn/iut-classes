## Block 1: fallacy drill (10 min)

Cover the answers, say the fallacy out loud, then check. These slogans are made up for practice. **Don't use them in the quiz.**

1. "9 out of 10 dentists recommend Brite."
2. "Still single? Maybe it's your breath. Try FreshMint."
3. "The phone everyone at your university already has."
4. "Pure, natural, organic tobacco."
5. "World-famous striker drinks ZapCola. Shouldn't you?"
6. "Our cars are built like a mountain."
7. "Pre-owned luxury" (for used cars).
8. "Wear it and be happy."
9. "Since switching to SlimTea, Dilnoza lost 10 kg!"
10. "Only for those who have truly made it."
11. "The No.1 miracle cream in all of Central Asia."
12. "Real men drive diesel."

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **Misleading statistics**: 9 out of how many asked, who funded it, and recommend it over what?
2. **Scare tactics**: shame and fear of being alone.
3. **Popular appeal / bandwagon**.
4. **Naturalistic fallacy**: "natural" doesn't mean safe.
5. **Inappropriate authority** (+ snob appeal): a footballer isn't a nutrition expert.
6. **False analogy**: a mountain lasts forever, a car loses value in 5–10 years.
7. **Euphemism**: it's a used car.
8. **Amphiboly**: does wearing it *cause* happiness, or are these two separate ideas?
9. **Questionable cause** (+ hasty generalization from one case).
10. **Snob appeal**.
11. **Hyperbole**.
12. **Hasty generalization / stereotype** about men.

</details>

Score yourself. If you got under 9, reread the table in section 3 of the 6-1 summary before Block 2.

## Block 2: Quiz 4 Q1, a local ad (2 pts, 10 min)

**Rule from the lecture [28:28]:** it has to be something you actually saw locally, not from ChatGPT or a random search.

Places to look tonight, fast:
- **Your phone:** Telegram channels you already follow (shops, Uzum Market, Korzinka, Texnomart, telecoms like
  Beeline, Ucell, Mobiuz and Uzmobile, banks like Uzum, Click or Payme, food delivery). Screenshot or copy the post link.
- **Instagram or YouTube pre-rolls** from local brands.
- **On your run route:** a billboard, a metro poster or a shop window. A photo from tonight counts.
- Packaging at home, like a drink, snack or cosmetics label ("natural", "No.1", "doctors recommend").

Write it yourself in this shape:
> *[What the ad is, where you saw it]. It uses [fallacy name], because [what in the ad does it] — [why that's a problem / what it wants you to believe].*

Two fallacies spotted with a clear "because" beats one vague one. **Attach the photo or screenshot** (up to 2 files) or paste the link.

## Block 3: Quiz 4 Q2, media analysis (3 pts, 20 min)

This is worth more, so spend the time here.

**Pick one local news item** (Kun.uz, Gazeta.uz, Daryo, Podrobno.uz, Spot.uz, UzDaily, a TV news clip, or a local blogger's
report). Pick something with a clear angle, such as prices, a new law, a scandal, construction, a sports result or a
viral incident. Bland press releases are harder to analyse. **Copy the link**, because it goes in the answer.

Answer **at least 6 of the 11**, each in a couple of real sentences (the total must be at least 3–4 sentences, but aim for more).
Lecture hooks you can bring in (they show you watched it):

| Question | Hook from the lecture |
|---|---|
| 1. Who created it? | The outlet, the reporter, **who owns or funds it** (filter 1: ownership) |
| 2. Purpose? | Inform, sell, persuade or entertain? Is advertising nearby (filter 2)? |
| 3. Attention techniques? | Headline wording, photo, **sensationalism**, doom and gloom, scare tactics |
| 4. Values / point of view? | Whose view gets quoted? **Official sources only** (filter 3)? |
| 5–6. Your reaction and why | Be honest: did the headline steer your feeling before you read the facts? |
| 7. Reasonable interpretation? | Did you check the original source or another outlet? |
| 8. Different readers? | Someone affected vs an outsider, Uzbek- vs Russian-language audiences, young vs old |
| 9. Biased? | Hasty generalizations, one side missing, **echo chamber** |
| 10. Good reasoning and facts? | Are numbers sourced? Is there "research shows…" without a link (scientific-reporting checklist)? |
| 11. Effects? | On individuals (fear, spending) and on society (trust, polarisation) |

Tip: answer 7–8 questions instead of 6 for a safety margin, and number them so the grader can count.
