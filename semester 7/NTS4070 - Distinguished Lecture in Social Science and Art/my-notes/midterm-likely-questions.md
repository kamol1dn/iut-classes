  (2) application: a sentence, real-life event or quote, and you name what it is ("which fallacy is this?").
- **Split:** ~9 questions on **fallacies** (Lecture 4) · ~18 on **Lectures 1, 5, 6.1, 7** (critical thinking,
  arguments, advertising, science) · ~3 on **Lectures 2, 3, 6.2** (reason & emotion, cognitive errors, media).
- In no other lecture does he say "this will be on the midterm". The ★ cards in the flashcard app are the
  points he **stressed** instead: his own examples, repeating himself, "pause and think", or minutes spent on one slide.

My guess at his method: he writes the application questions from **his own examples in the VODs**, so those
are the ones to know cold.

## Fallacies (~9 questions): learn the confusable pairs

| His example | Answer | Not this |
|---|---|---|
| "Mamdani supports socialism, socialism failed in the USSR" [26:38] | **Straw man** | red herring |
| AI CEOs' doom talk; parents' "while I pay your tuition…" [18:24] | **Appeal to force / scare tactics** | appeal to pity |
| Lucky jacket / lucky route to the exam [36:53] | **Questionable cause (post hoc)** | slippery slope |
| "Allow it once and your child ends up a criminal"; "listen to students once → strikes" [38:54–40:57] | **Slippery slope** | questionable cause |
| Celebrity / blogger on a podcast talking politics or money [22:31] | **Inappropriate appeal to authority** | snob appeal |
| Celebrity *selling a product* ("use what the stars use") [34:53] | **Snob appeal / popular appeal** | appeal to authority |
| "UFOs don't exist since nobody proved they do" [22:31–24:34] | **Appeal to ignorance** (exception: courts, innocent until proven guilty) | begging the question |
| "Abusive men in my life, so all men are abusers" [24:34] | **Hasty generalisation** | division |
| "Canadians are nice, so Derek is nice"; "many criminals from Russia, so Russians are bad" [14:21] | **Division** / generalisation he added himself | composition |
| "Wear it and be happy" [10:19] | **Amphiboly** (ambiguous grammar) | equivocation |

## Lectures 1, 5, 6.1, 7 (~18 questions)

**Lecture 1: Critical thinking**
- Critical thinking = a collection of skills: **how** to think, not **what** to think [18:23]
- Perry's stages, especially **relativism** ("no right or wrong answers") [20:25–24:32]
- Three-tier model: **experience → interpretation → analysis**, with his job-interview and young-voters examples [32:50]
- Barriers: **resistance vs narrow-mindedness** [36:53–38:53]; Milgram/Eichmann lesson: people follow authority uncritically [14:22]

**Lecture 5: Arguments**
- **Issue** = what's in question; wallet example: "what happened to it" vs "who stole it" [12:22–16:27]
- **Rhetoric** (persuade) vs **argument** (discover the truth) [16:27]
- Premise types: **descriptive, prescriptive** (value), analogical, definitional; **explanation ≠ argument** [22:30–26:35]
- **Indicators:** therefore / thus / so = conclusion · since / because / given that = premise [28:40]
- What a **proposition** is (he had you pause and make one) [20:29]

**Lecture 6.1: Advertising**
- Slide definitions: **amphiboly** (uncertain grammar), **euphemism** (substituting words), **hyperbole** (exaggeration) [22:20–24:24]
- Scenarios: celebrity outside their field, **"100% natural"** (naturalistic fallacy), "everyone has one" (popular appeal) [20:17]
- **Product placement**: logos on football shirts, car brands in films [14:14]; scare tactics, the AI CEOs again [18:14]

**Lecture 7: Science**
- Five assumptions: **empiricism, objectivity, materialism, predictability, unity** [04:05–08:08]
- Good hypothesis: **relevant, consistent with established theories, simple, falsifiable, predictive power**
- **Falsifiability, not confirmation**: "all swans are white" [30:48]
- **Kuhn:** paradigm → normal science → scientific revolution (Copernicus) [49:10]
- **Publish or perish** (4 minutes on it, IUT's paper targets) [43:05]; pseudoscience: astrology, numerology [34:52]
