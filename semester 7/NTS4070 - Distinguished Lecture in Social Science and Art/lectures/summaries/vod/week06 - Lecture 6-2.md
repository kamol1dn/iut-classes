## Opening clip: *Manufacturing Consent* [00:20]

A short video on **Herman & Chomsky (1988)**: media don't check power, they *manufacture consent*. The **five filters**:

| # | Filter | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ownership** | media firms are profit-driven corporations, so critical journalism comes second |
| 2 | **Advertising** | advertisers pay the gap, and what they buy is **you**, the audience |
| 3 | **Sourcing / the establishment** | governments and corporations feed scoops and experts. Challenge them and you lose access |
| 4 | **Flak** | inconvenient stories, sources and whistleblowers get discredited |
| 5 | **A common enemy** | communism, terrorists, immigrants: a bogeyman unites opinion |

His takeaway: journalists need salaries, so someone always funds media (the government, corporations or
advertisers). We consume it for free, so we need **media literacy** [05:10].

## 1. What mass media is [07:00]

- **Mass media** is communication designed to reach and influence very large audiences, as opposed to niche media.
  Today that includes the internet, social media and **bloggers**, who can become mass media themselves.
- **Credibility of news coverage** [08:14]: media can expose wrongdoing. The book's example is
  **John D. Rockefeller**, whose oil monopoly (built through cartels with suppliers) was exposed by journalists,
  after which the government broke up the company.
- **Sensationalism and news as entertainment** [10:30]: people want a **dopamine hit**, like celebrities, shock
  videos and Shorts or Reels, and avoid in-depth analysis.
- **Bias in the news** [12:22]: outlets skew towards what their followers want.
- **Shallow analysis**: competition for attention pushes even big outlets towards short-form content.

## 2. Evaluating scientific reporting [14:28]

"Science" is a slippery slope, because hearing *research shows…* makes people switch off their critical thinking. Ask:

1. **Who is making the claim?** Which site, which reporter?
2. **What are the credentials and funding of the source?** Results can be skewed by funders. His example:
   upbeat AI research funded by Altman, Amodei or Musk [16:32].
3. **What is the reporter's background?**
4. **Where was the study first published?** Kun.uz, BBC and CNN report on papers, so go to the original
   and check that the journal is credible.
5. **How are other experts responding?** Is it one-sided, or broadly agreed?
6. **Is the report biased?** Has the outlet exaggerated the paper to suit its audience?

## 3. Media literacy and analysis [18:50]

**Media literacy** is not just knowing *how to use* media. It's the ability to **understand and critically analyse
its influence**, and the same goes for social-media and AI literacy.

The analysis questions (**use these for the task**):

1. Who created the message?
2. What is its purpose?
3. What techniques attract and hold attention (scare tactics, doom-and-gloom)?
4. What values and points of view are represented (political, moral)?
5. What was your reaction?
6. Why did it make you feel that way?
7. Is your interpretation reasonable and well-informed?
8. How might different people interpret it?
9. Is it biased (hasty generalizations, skewed towards or against groups)?
10. Is it backed by good reasoning and facts? Check the premises and sources.
11. What are its possible effects on individuals and society?

**Echo chambers** [23:00]: if you only follow Uzbek bloggers, or only Russian-language channels, or only Western
corporate media (BBC, CNN, NYT), you see one side. Seek out alternative media, including unregistered bloggers and
foreign coverage of Uzbekistan, to get the other side of the coin.
