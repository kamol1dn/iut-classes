## Cold open: what propaganda is [00:03]

A montage of definitions and famous clips:

- Propaganda is **"political brainwashing"**, **"a calculated attack on the complexity of other people's minds"**,
  **"the invisible hand"**, **"getting you to submit without realizing consciously that you're submitting"** [00:51–1:07].
- **"Wherever there is power, there is propaganda."** If we grow up surrounded only by propaganda, how do we know what
  is true?
- **Repetition**: *"you've got to keep repeating things over and over and over again for the truth to sink in"*
  (George W. Bush, 2005) [01:41], then the Brexit slogan **"take back control … 350 million pounds"** repeated three times [01:49].
- **"Propaganda is essential for the modern state."** "The power of art is a dangerous thing" [02:06].
- **The central question** [02:06–03:40]: we let ourselves be manipulated by **fictions as though they were
  uncontestable truths**. How have our powers of reason so often been **overtaken by the irrational**?
- **Language** gave humans a new power, **grasping the symbolic layer of reality**. **Magic, art and religion share the
  same roots** [03:40].

## 1. Origins: the cave as the first propaganda [04:09]

**El Castillo cave** (northern Spain), famous for its **hand stencils**:

- The hand stencil is **"probably the first symbolic piece of art"** that represents something in the real world. It
  was made with care, and the location was chosen deliberately. **"Not art for art's sake. This is meaningful art."**
- The archaeologist says these are **Neanderthal hands, about 65,000 years old** [04:31]. That makes it **"the origin
  of symbolic culture"** and of art. **"Symbolic communication lies at the heart of propaganda."** It's the toolkit
  propaganda was formed from.
- **Technique**: put a hand on the wall and blow pigment over it (spat or through a tube). That gives a **negative
  stencil**, **"the same method that modern graffiti artists use"** [05:10].
- About **40 stencils** are clustered in one area, so maybe it was **public art** for gatherings or rituals.
- **Great propaganda exploits your need for belief and your appetite for "a magical form of empowerment"** [05:36].
- The cave is **"like a modern cathedral"** [06:04]: stalagmites, tallow lamps, flickering shadows, the place the spirits
  lived. **The shaman** leads you into the dark among pictures of animal spirits. **The caves may have been an elaborate
  way of convincing people of the shaman's power. "That's propaganda."** [06:22]
- So: **"The power of the image has been with us from the beginning."** Is that why we're so easily seduced?

### The film's purpose [07:04]

Clip: *"You are fake news."* Today we're **bombarded with loaded images** from computers and phones, **"as if the
dystopian fiction of George Orwell has become our daily reality"** [07:17]. When truth is attacked, knowledge debased,

## 2. Fake news and storytelling [08:22]

**Craig Silverman** (BuzzFeed News) explains where the term **fake news** came from:

- He first met it in **late summer/fall 2014** [08:52]: stories that were **100 % false but looked like news websites**.
- **2016 US election**: young men and teenagers in the **former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia** ran **more than 150
  pro-Trump sites** [09:15]. Their motive was **money** (ad revenue), not ideology.
- Hit fakes: **"the Pope endorsed Trump"** and **"Hillary Clinton approved a sale of weapons to ISIS"** [09:40].
- **Storytelling is embedded in propaganda.** **"Good propaganda is a good story… There is an enemy. There is a
  hero."** [10:15] Propaganda through the centuries has taken **specific symbols and given them supernatural power**.

## 3. Alexander the Great: the cult of personality [10:45]

- Alexander was **one of the first leaders to see how his presence should be symbolized**, a candidate for "one of the
  greatest propagandists ever". **"He became a god. That's the point. And I think he did that consciously."**
- The problem: **a vast empire, and you can't be everywhere**. The solution: **statues of Alexander** and **coins with
  his head** that travel "to the ends of the world" carrying the message **"believe in me and everything's going to be
  fine"** [11:26].
- Three key claims [11:41–11:56]:
  - **"Propaganda wants to be everywhere."**
  - **It's only propaganda when it's part of a larger effort of "engineering reality".**
  - **"When we don't think it's propaganda, it works the best as propaganda."**

## 4. Propaganda of omission: the American West and Kent Monkman [11:56]

- **"The most insidious propaganda is the propaganda of omission."** 19th-century **landscape paintings of the American
  West** have a **"sublime light"** but carry an ideology: the West as **"a big piece of empty real estate"** [12:16].
  The Indigenous people living there are left out.
- **Kent Monkman** (Cree artist) goes back to the **"zenith of propaganda painting in the 19th century"**, **history
  painting**, which he calls the most sophisticated form because brushstroke, surface and content all **serve a larger
  purpose** [12:42]. He uses that **authority of history painting** to tell **Indigenous histories** instead: a
  **counter-propaganda**.
- His painting ***The Scoop*** [13:38] shows the **"Sixties Scoop"**: in **1960s Canada**, Indigenous children were taken
  from their families and adopted into non-Indigenous families or put in **residential schools**.
  - **"The forcible transfer of children from one cultural group to another is the definition of genocide."** [13:55]
  - The **Mountie** is a friendly postcard symbol of Canada. **In fact, they carried out the policy** [14:01].
  - Goal: **"dismantle the propaganda of the government selling people a myth, a story, a lie about themselves"** [14:20].

## 5. Orwell's *1984* as a warning system [15:06]

- *1984* is a dystopian novel of a totalitarian society (**Room 101**, **Big Brother**). It has never been out of print,
  and it **shot back up the best-seller lists** in 2017.
- Spliced together: Trump's 2015 line on Mexican migrants (**"they're bringing drugs… crime… rapists"**) [15:29], the
  *1984* film's **"we are at war with Eurasia"** [15:31], and Bush's **"axis of evil"** (2002) [15:43].
- People now read Orwell **"as a warning system… like a medical directory: what's wrong with us?"** [15:56] *Could we be
  made to hate somebody at the flip of an ideological switch?* A reader says it used to describe "another world" and
  now describes **"the world I was in"**.
- Clips: **"What you're seeing and what you're reading is not what's happening"** (Trump), **"alternative facts"**
  (Kellyanne Conway, 2017) [16:43], **"Truth isn't truth"** (Rudy Giuliani, 2018) [16:45]. That leads into "reject the
  evidence, but have faith in magical mythologies".

## 6. The Church: where the word comes from [17:00]

- **"Arguably the most effective propagandist in history has been the Church."**
- **The word**: from Latin, originally meaning **"to plant"** [17:17]. The Catholic Church used it for
  **evangelization**: **you plant or seed the faith**. *(Background, not in the video: the name comes from the
  **Congregatio de Propaganda Fide**, the "Congregation for Propagating the Faith", set up by the papacy in 1622.)*
- **The Reformation as a media war** [17:40]: around **200 printing presses** in Europe at the start of the 16th century
  became **"the heavy artillery of the propaganda war"**.
  - **Martin Luther** understood the press. **The first bestseller was one of his tracts against the sale of
    indulgences** [17:55].
  - He paired his sermons with **woodcuts by really good artists** that attacked the Catholic establishment [18:15].
- **The Counter-Reformation** [18:34]: the popes made the Church's whole means of communication **more accessible to
  ordinary people**: painting, sculpture, architecture, music. That **"gave rise to the glory that is the Baroque"** [19:11].
- **"The heart of any propaganda campaign is an emotional appeal."** **Baroque = manipulating the senses, "playing to the
  heart rather than to the mind."** [19:23]

## 7. The Che Guevara poster: Jim Fitzpatrick [19:42]

**Jim Fitzpatrick**, creator of the **1968 red-and-black Che Guevara poster** (*Viva Che*):

- **He met Che** while working as a barman in a pub in **Kilkee**, Ireland. Che was stuck for about two days by fog on a
  stopover at **Shannon Airport** [20:04–20:25]. For Fitzpatrick, a hero "of the revolution".
- **Simplification**: many early versions were "overcomplicated", so he kept simplifying [20:54].
- **The trigger**: Che was **executed as a prisoner of war** (Bolivia, 1967). His body was displayed in a way that made
  him **"look like the dead Christ"** [21:27], and there was an attempt to **"disappear" him**. That resonates in Ireland
  and recalls **Argentina's Dirty War**.
- **"When I did the Che Guevara image, I did that deliberately as a piece of propaganda."** He made it **as simple as
  possible so anybody could copy it** and declared it **copyright-free "for all revolutionary and leftist use"** [22:05].
- It **spread "of its own accord"**: toilets in Tokyo, endless T-shirts [22:20]. Nobody believed he made it. **"Kind of
  accidentally, I created one of the most iconic images of all time"** [22:42]. He feels he "commemorated a great man and
  made him live again".

## 8. World War I and the two lessons [23:26]

- **Total war**: whole populations against whole populations. Governments had to **justify war, for the first time on a
  major scale**. Propaganda was used by **all belligerents**, through print and posters and also the **new technology,
  cinema** [24:20].
- **After the war, many atrocity stories were seen as untrue** [24:48]. So in **Britain and America, propaganda became
  "firmly associated with lies and falsehood"** [25:01].
- **Germany, Italy, Russia / the Soviet Union learned the opposite lesson**: propaganda isn't necessarily negative. It's a
  tool to use [25:06].

## 9. Stalin and the psychology of believing [25:53]

- ***The Fall of Berlin*** (1949) [25:53]: an actor who became **a national treasure** played **Stalin** in several films
  as a **wise, benevolent figure who sacrifices everything** for his country. The film is **"the apotheosis"** of the
  leader. Crowd: **"We love Stalin!"** [26:33]
- **Propaganda disables intelligence** [26:58]: it's something we **"cannot bring our full intelligence to bear on"**.
  Looking back, **"How could I have believed that?"** is **like the end of a love affair** [27:13].
- **The disillusionment over Stalin** shows **how much selective attention was needed to keep believing** [27:39].
  People had actively, if unconsciously, attacked their own capacity for perception and thought. There is **"an active
  part of oneself that can determinedly not know what one knows."**
- **"Propaganda is based on how you stop yourself knowing things."** [28:08]

## 10. North Korea and "our" propaganda: Morten Traavik [28:59]

- Traavik says **much of what Western media tell us about North Korea is itself propaganda** [29:01]. People imagine
  **"Mordor… hell on earth"**, and "many people make a living out of selling this narrative" [29:31].
- **Under dictatorship, people censor themselves**, because we're "comfortable animals" who want to stay out of trouble
  [29:50].
- **The paradox**: many North Koreans **know what is propaganda and what isn't**. The **staging is so total and so
  obvious** that a **"parallel existence"**, basically normal life, runs beneath it **as long as you pay lip service**
  [30:29]. People from many totalitarian systems say the same: *we waved our flags, went to the May Day parade, and kept
  our sanity.*
- **The twist** [31:09]: to us, North Korean propaganda is **"quaint and kitsch"**. But we're subject to **subtler forms
  all the time**, and it's a **misconception that we're less exposed** than the average North Korean.

## 11. Propaganda is what "other people" consume: Astra [31:43]

- **Default definition: "Propaganda is something other people consume."** Nobody says "I love propaganda". We flatter
  ourselves [31:43].
- Standard histories list the **Catholic Church, the Soviet Union, Nazi propaganda**, and **leave a lot out**.
- **Patriarchal and sexist messages**: there's **no "Bureau of Male Affairs"** sending them from above. They're "in the
  ether", and **we call them stereotypes or bias, not propaganda** [32:45]. Example: ***The Bachelor*** ("will you accept
  this rose?").
- **The most propagandistic thing in North American culture is the most mainstream culture**: Hollywood films (***She's
  All That***), home-improvement and real-estate shows (***House Hunters***). They present an **idealized, consumeristic
  life** [33:15–33:18].
- **"The most successful propaganda ever… says: what you really want is to be rich."** [33:35] Capitalism breeds **envy**:
  it looks as if **1 % live life as it should be lived** and everyone else suffers [33:41]. **"We've been sold the wrong
  picture of a good life."** [34:01]

## 13. The Obama "Hope" poster [37:12]

- **"It's really bad when … just telling the truth is revolutionary."** Street art without permission is risky: he's
  been **arrested 18 times**, and as a **type 1 diabetic** he is often denied insulin in jail [37:39–37:48]. But street art
  **meets people "where they live their daily lives"**.
- **"I even call my work propaganda."** The word has a negative connotation, **"but I don't think it has to be
  negative."** He uses the **tools of propaganda in a socially responsible way** [38:27].
- The poster was a **grassroots** piece that **originally said "PROGRESS"** [38:52]. It went **viral**, and the **Obama
  campaign asked him to use "HOPE" or "CHANGE"** [39:09]. He liked **hope**: under Bush many felt hopeless, and
  **"without hope, people don't act"** [39:13].
- **Design choices**: the campaign's branding was good but lacked **human connection**. He made Obama look **patriotic,
  visionary and legitimate**, which mattered because Obama was **unknown and unproven**. A "two-dimensional sculpture" of

  **"American rather than African-American"** [40:16].

## 14. Ai Weiwei: the individual against the state [40:56]

- **Chairman Mao**: **"we not only control the gun, but also the pen."** [40:56]

- Narrator: **"the most powerful political artist in the world"**, a critic of the Chinese authoritarian regime, now **in

- **"Art functions like a cancer in communist society. It destroys every single cell. That's why I'm seen as the most
  dangerous person for the state."** [42:00]
- **Erased authorship** [42:39]: in totalitarian regimes, even **Cultural Revolution posters** were made by individuals,

- He started a **blog** [43:00] and was **beaten by police, causing a brain hemorrhage** [43:25], just for **"freely

- **"All art is propaganda, but it's not propaganda from a powerful state, but rather from the heart of an
  individual."** [44:17] **"The essential power of art is a subversive power. Art is about freedom."** [44:39]

## 15. Transcendence and the Nazis: Leni Riefenstahl [45:35]

- The Nazis **knew propaganda's potential because they were sensitive to the power of art. Art moved them** [45:35].
- **Transcendence** [45:54]: coming **out of your own little life and feeling one with something larger**. It's
  **"one of the most powerful emotions we can have, and it can be very dangerous."** It works through music, painting
  and especially **film**.
- **Leni Riefenstahl** [46:33]. The rally film shown is almost certainly ***Triumph of the Will*** (1935, Nuremberg
  rally); the video doesn't give the title. She had **a huge budget**, rare for any director. It is **"one of the most
  impressive and most effective propaganda films"** of the Nazis.
- **The art-vs-politics problem**: she was used by the Nazis and wanted to take part, **but "she was still a great
  artist"**. **"Incredibly modern camera paths or cuts"** [47:08]. It's **more than a documentary and more than common
  propaganda: "almost a piece of propaganda art."** [47:14]
- It was all **"operatic because it came out of Wagner"** [47:31].

## 16. Ritual, religion and the whole person [47:52]

- It used **"components of information in a manipulative way"** to produce **an almost religious belief in a system**.
- **Most Germans were still churchgoers** [48:07]. The Nazis' **torch-lit night processions** borrowed from
  **candle-lit religious processions** [48:12].
- **Engage every sense** [48:35]: visuals, buildings and backdrops, **incense** (smell), the body and choreographed
  processions, music. It's present in **organized religion and in quasi-religious ideologies like Nazism**. It works
  because it **involves the entire person** and ties **strong sensual responses to "ultimate truths"**. In those moments
  **"my senses, my life, and eternal truth become a single thing"** [49:16]. *"Very beautiful, very moving, very
  dangerous if it's misused."*
- **A Christian priest's defence** [49:37]: with choir, incense, masterpieces in a church, *"I know what I'm doing"*. He
  does it **not to manipulate**, but because it brings people closer to the mystery of **a spiritual God who becomes a
  physical human being**. **"If we're all wrong, it has at least been innocent manipulation."** [50:32]

## 19. Charlie Hebdo: humour as a weapon [56:40]

- **Defence against "obsessed with Islam"** [58:13]: pick covers at random and most are about **French politics**. They
  comment on the news, and when Islam/Islamism is in the news, they cover it.
- **His propaganda claim** [59:01]: **"Islamism has managed to make us believe that when we attack its discourse, we
  attack a whole community."**
- **Faith vs religion** [59:13–59:22]:

| | Meaning (his framing) |
|---|---|
| **Faith** | individual, personal |
| **Religion** | **"the social and political organization of faith."** So **religion is political**, and today it's "in political conquest" |

  Conclusion: since it's politics, **"we must be able to criticize, attack, mock, insult religion"** [59:55].

## 20. The Cold War and the bomb [1:00:16]

- **"Paranoia, xenophobia, irrational fear. These are the pillars of propaganda that have led us into our deadliest
  conflicts."** [1:00:16]
- **The Cold War**: a new kind of war, **fueled by unrelenting propaganda on all sides**, built on the constant threat
  of **instant global annihilation** [1:00:28].
- **Hiroshima and Nagasaki** [1:00:49]: the bombing **without warning was "one of the most potent forms of propaganda
  at the time", and explicitly acknowledged as such**.
  - **Chilling detail** [1:01:09]: the US military **kept certain Japanese cities free of conventional bombing** so

  - Damage-survey footage from "Zero Point" [1:01:25]. The **propagandistic effect** was always in mind. **The message

- Soviet counter-footage: **"behind the so-called Statue of Liberty"**, factories behind barbed wire and machine guns

## For the debate

Relevant to **mass-media censorship**, **IT and democracy**, **social media and children**, **capitalism** and **AI dystopia**:

- **Against censorship**: Charlie Hebdo's line that religion is political, so it must be open to criticism and mockery;
  Ai Weiwei's state violence for speaking freely; Mao's "we control the gun and the pen". The worry is **who decides
  what's propaganda**.
- **For regulation or media literacy**: the Macedonian fake-news farms were **run for money**, so the incentive is
  economic. Platforms spread lies faster than corrections; Silverman's examples are concrete and citeable.
- **Media literacy over bans**: Traavik's point that people under dictatorship often *know* it's propaganda, and Astra's
  point that the subtle, mainstream kind is the dangerous one. Banning the obvious kind leaves the invisible kind untouched.
- **Capitalism**: "the most successful propaganda ever says *you want to be rich*" and "we've been sold the wrong
  picture of a good life". Opponents will say this is opinion, not data, so pair it with statistics.
- **Careful**: speakers' figures ("a bomb every 20 minutes for eight years", the 65,000-year date) are their claims.
  Check them before quoting as facts.
