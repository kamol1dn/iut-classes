## Ch.10: Marketing & Advertising (6.1)

- **Marketing research** identifies a target market and checks it against customer desires. Its tools are
  **surveys**, **observation** (buying patterns) and **experimentation** (cause and effect).
- **Strategic plan**: how an organization deploys its resources to reach a goal. Marketing's goals include brand recognition,
  impulse buying and a longer purchase experience.
- **Advertising** aims to create awareness, inform, and motivate demand. **Product placement** means
  real products shown in fiction, media and sport, usually without being noticed.
- **Evaluating ads** means spotting the fallacies from Ch.5: scare tactics, questionable cause, popular appeal /
  group pressure, snob appeal, appeal to inappropriate authority, hasty generalization,
  **amphiboly** (ambiguous grammar), naturalistic fallacy, **euphemism**, **hyperbole**, analogy,
  and misused logic and statistics.

## Ch.11: Mass Media (6.2)

- **Mass media**: communication designed to reach and influence very large audiences. Issues covered:
  credibility, **sensationalism / news as entertainment**, **bias**, and shallow analysis. The intro
  topic is **media dependence**.
- **Evaluating science reporting** asks: who makes the claim, what their credentials and funding are, what the reporter's background is,
  where the study was first published, how other experts respond, and whether it is biased.
- **Media literacy**: the ability to understand and critically analyse media influence. The question set:
  who created it, the purpose, the attention techniques, the values represented, your reaction and why,
  whether your reading is reasonable, how others might read it, bias, the reasoning and facts behind it, and its effects
  on individuals and society.

## Ch.12: Science (7)

- **Science** reasons from observable, measurable facts to hypotheses to testable explanations.
  It rests on five assumptions: **empiricism**, **objectivity**, **materialism**, **predictability**, **unity**.
- **Limits of science**: Descartes (we only have the *idea* of an outside world), empiricism restricts science
  to the observable, observation can't prove a *necessary* causal link (Hume), perception depends on the brain,
  and quantum mechanics.
- **Science and religion** has four positions: science wins, religion wins, separate realms, compatible and complementary.
- **Scientific method**: identify the problem → initial hypothesis → gather information and refine → test → evaluate.
- **Judging a hypothesis**: is it relevant, consistent with established theory, the **simplest**, **testable and
  falsifiable**, and predictive? **Pseudoscience** (astrology, numerology, psychic healing) only masquerades as
  science and leans on emotion, superstition and rhetoric.
- **Research designs**: experimental, correlational, survey, ethnographic, grounded theory, narrative,
  action, mixed methods.
- **Ethics**: informed consent, honest reporting, citation, "publish or perish", the neutrality of science.
- **Thomas Kuhn**: science is neither purely objective nor steadily progressive. **Normal science** builds on
  accepted achievements. A **paradigm** is the accepted view of the world and how to study it. A **scientific
  revolution** is a paradigm shift.
