## Format (slide 3)

1. **The draw.** At the start, a draw picks the proposition team (for the motion) and the opposition team (against it).
   You don't know your side until you're in the room, so **prepare both sides** of your topic. He said the same in week 6
   ([Debate survey](vod/week06%20-%20Debate%20survey.md): a coin toss on the day).
2. **Three speeches per team, 3–4 minutes each.** The slide says "per person", so three different speakers.
3. **One question** to the other team.
4. **One answer / defence, 2–3 minutes**: answering the question you're asked.

*(my note: per team, 3 × (3–4 min) + (2–3 min) = **11–15 minutes**, plus the time to ask the question, which fits the 15-minute
allowance only if speeches stay short. Rehearse with a timer and aim for 3 minutes per speech. The slide doesn't say whose
turn comes first, whether the question comes after all six speeches, or who asks and who answers. With teams of 4–6 (week 6
video), three speakers plus a questioner and a defender is a natural split, but that's my guess, not his rule.)*

## Grading (slide 4)

Five criteria, in the deck's order. No weights are given, so treat them as equal until he says otherwise.

| Criterion | What earns it |
|---|---|
| **Understanding of topic** | In-depth knowledge of the topic, presented forcefully and convincingly |
| **Organization of arguments** | Every argument tied to one idea (a premise) and organised in a tight, logical way |
| **Use of facts / statistics** | Each major point supported by several relevant facts, statistics and/or examples |
| **Defense** | Answers that are accurate, relevant and strong |
| **Presentation style** | Tone of voice, clarity of expression, respect for the other team, professional manners |

*(my note: two of these are the midterm material in practice. "Organization of arguments" is the premise → conclusion
structure from Lecture 5 (Ch.6), and a fallacy from Lecture 4 (Ch.5) in your own speech is the easiest point to lose under
"Defense". "Use of facts" rewards sources you can name: Ch.11–12's questions about who made a claim and where it was published apply.)*
