## 1. The role of marketing in business [02:50]

- Businesses chase **profit**, and in a consumer culture **marketing is the most critical component**. Big
  corporations often only design and market, and **outsource manufacturing** to countries like
  Uzbekistan, China or Vietnam. Selling is the real job.
- **Marketing research** identifies a target or niche market and checks whether it matches customer desires
  (their problems, needs, "what drives people") [04:50]. Three methods:

  | Method | What it is | His examples |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Surveys** | collecting information | online, in-store, street, **focus groups** |
  | **Observation** | monitoring buying patterns | big data, website click paths, how long a decision takes |
  | **Experimentation** | measuring **cause and effect** | how packaging, delivery or the call centre changes buying |

- A **strategic plan** is how an organization deploys its resources to reach a goal [08:20]. Three strategies:
  - **Brand recognition**: a logo, colours or mission recognisable even by a child.
  - **Motivating impulse buying**: discounts, too many options, short decision windows.
  - **Lengthening the purchase experience**: more time in the shop means more purchases. His example is the
    **Havas / Korzinka layout**, where the bread is at the entrance and the checkout is at the far end,
    "by design, by purpose" [11:00].

## 2. Advertising [12:13]

- Three purposes: **create product awareness**, **inform** about features, and **motivate demand** (the main one).
- **Traditional** advertising is ads you know are ads: TV breaks, cinema pre-rolls, billboards.
- **Product placement** is real products inside fiction, media or sport, **usually unnoticed** [14:14].
  Examples: GM, German or Toyota cars in American films, sponsor logos on football shirts.

| **Snob appeal** | linking the product to attractive, strong or successful people | alcohol ads showing happy, fun-loving drinkers |
| **Appeal to inappropriate authority** | a celebrity, athlete or scientist endorsing outside their field | — |
| **Hasty generalization** | ads that **reinforce stereotypes** about women or men | — |
| **Amphiboly** | ambiguous grammar, so the company can later claim it meant something else | — |
| **Naturalistic fallacy** | "natural" means good | "natural tobacco" cigarettes |
| **Euphemism** | softer words for something unpleasant, leading to the same conclusion | — |
