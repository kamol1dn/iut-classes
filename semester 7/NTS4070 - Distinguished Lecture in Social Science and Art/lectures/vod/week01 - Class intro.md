## His argument for why this course matters

Worth noting because it signals what he will reward in debates and the midterm.

He frames the course against the arrival of large language models. His claim:
now that hard skills can be obtained "through these generative tools," the open
question is what the human role is — and the durable differentiator becomes
judgment, critical reflection, and people skills, which he argues these tools
cannot supply for you.

From that he draws a direct instruction: **do not use AI tools for this course.**
He puts it as opportunity rather than prohibition — final year is likely the last
stretch of life with time to sit, read, and think for its own sake, before work
and family crowd it out.

## Definitions he set up

He opens by asking students to separate three terms that get conflated:

- **Social sciences** — study the *interactions* between humans: individuals, communities, societies, how they behave and affect their environment. Sociology, economics, anthropology, politics, psychology.
- **Humanities** — also about humans, but study their *activities and products*: history, culture, language, literature.
- **Arts** — a subset of the humanities; a mode of expression using skill and imagination to create works.

He pauses twice and asks students to stop the video and think — once on this
distinction, once on *why an engineering student needs this subject at all*.
Both are plausible quiz or midterm material.
