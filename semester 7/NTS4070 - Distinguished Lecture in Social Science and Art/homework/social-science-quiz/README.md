## The three questions

1. **What new and surprising information did you learn** from these three lectures?
2. **What challenged your mind?**
3. **What impact did it have on you?** Did you decide to investigate further, change something or take an action,
   or did it change something in your mind?

## How to write a good answer

- **Touch all three lectures.** The questions say "these three lectures", so a reflection on only one looks like
  you watched only one. One concrete idea from each is enough.
- **Name the idea, then react to it.** For example: "Heidegger says technology is not neutral. That surprised me
  because …". A named concept (*standing-reserve*, *the Orient as the West's "Other"*, *propaganda through art*)
  shows you watched; your reaction is what gets graded.
- **Q2 wants friction**: something you disagreed with, found hard, or that conflicted with what you believed before.
  "Nothing challenged me" is a weak answer.
- **Q3 wants something specific**: what you looked up afterwards, how you now see a phone, the news, a film or a
  poster differently, or a link to your own life in Uzbekistan or your field (computer science and AI fit
  Heidegger well).
- A few sentences per question in plain language is the scale. Earlier quizzes in this course gave 2–5 points
  for answers of that size.
