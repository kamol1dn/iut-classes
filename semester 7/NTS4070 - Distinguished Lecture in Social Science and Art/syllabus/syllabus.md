## Course description

The aim of this course is to introduce computer science and engineering students
to critical thinking skills and contemporary issues and debates in social
sciences, humanities, arts, and technology. By engaging in these discussions,
students will be able to broaden their horizons beyond their major fields, build
critical reflection skills, and foster creativity and ethical awareness.

## Course learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1. Learn the basics of critical thinking skills.
2. Analyze key concepts and perspectives from social sciences, humanities, arts and technology.
3. Critically evaluate the impact of science and technology on society, culture and human values.
4. Connect themes from art, history, sociology and philosophy to issues in computing and engineering.
5. Appreciate interdisciplinary thinking and its relevance to innovation.
6. Develop reflective and creative responses to complex societal challenges.

## Grading policy

Formal assessment is made on the basis of:

- **Attendance** — watching the weekly lectures and videos
- **Quiz** — related to lecture materials
- **Midterm** — based on the Critical Thinking chapters (weeks 1–7)
- **Debate (offline)** — group project, conducted in the last month of the semester; details to follow

### Grade allocation

| Attendance | Quiz | Midterm | Debate | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | 20% | 30% | 30% | 100% |

> **There is no final exam** — week 15 is listed as "Final week – no exam".
> Attendance is earned by **watching the weekly VODs inside their open window**,
> so a missed window is a lost 1/15 of a 20% component.
